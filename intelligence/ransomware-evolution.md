# Ransomware Evolution

## Executive Focus

- Ransomware operations increasingly combine intrusion, data theft, and business disruption.
- Encryption is one pressure mechanism among several, not the sole measure of impact.
- Resilience depends on limiting access, containing compromise, and recovering critical services.

## Evolution of Campaign Impact

### Rapid and Selective Encryption

- Shorter intervals between intrusion discovery and disruptive action.
- Targeting of high-value systems and shared infrastructure.
- Risk to hypervisors, management planes, and critical business applications.
- Need for early containment and tested workload-level recovery.

### Large-Scale Data Exfiltration

- Theft of sensitive, regulated, or commercially valuable information.
- Extortion based on threatened disclosure, even where systems can be restored.
- Downstream legal, privacy, contractual, and reputational exposure.
- Data classification, egress monitoring, and evidence-preservation requirements.

### Distributed Denial-of-Service (DDoS)

- Service disruption used as an additional pressure tactic.
- Potential impacts to customer-facing services and business communications.
- Coordination requirements across network, cloud, provider, and communications teams.

## Extortion Model

| Pressure mechanism | Primary business exposure | Preparedness focus |
|---|---|---|
| Encryption or destructive disruption | Operational downtime and recovery cost | Segmentation, continuity procedures, and independently tested recovery |
| Data-theft threats | Privacy, regulatory, contractual, and litigation risk | Data governance, legal assessment, and evidence handling |
| Customer or partner pressure | Trust erosion and commercial impact | Pre-approved stakeholder communications and account coordination |
| DDoS or public pressure | Availability and reputational impact | Provider escalation paths and resilient communications |

## Strategic Implications

- Measure resilience by business-service recovery, not by backup existence alone.
- Treat identity compromise and lateral movement as early warning signals.
- Separate backup administration and recovery credentials from production access.
- Prepare for simultaneous technical, legal, regulatory, and communications workstreams.
- Validate threat claims and attribution before making public or high-impact decisions.
